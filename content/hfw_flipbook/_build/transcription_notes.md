## p07 (printed 6) INTRODUCTION TO DRUG ADMINISTRATION OF AZITHROMYCIN - no figures
What is AZM? AZM means Azithromycin.
AZM benefits. Azithromycin is a broad-based spectrum antibiotic which is effective in the prevention and treatment of various bacterial infections including acute lower respiratory tract infections, skin and soft tissue and gastro-enteric infections. / It offers broad benefits including protection against malaria caused by Plasmodium falciparum. / Azithromycin reduces childhood mortality by preventing and treating childhood infections such as pneumonia, diarrhoea and malaria.
What is the target group for AZM: AZM drug administration is aimed protection children aged 1-59 months of age from childhood infections.
What is the delivery strategy? door-to-door approach by trained Community Drug Distributors (CDDs), under the supervision of the CDD supervisors, LGA, State and National teams. / The AZM administration data is collected using the Technology app (DIGIT HCM) installed in the devices of the CDDs.

## p08 (printed 7) same heading - no figures
What is AZM POS? AZM POS means Azithromycin Powdered Oral Suspension
Duration: AZM is administered every six-months (Bi-annually) to children 1 to 59 months of age.
Delivery platforms: AZM MDA will leverage on the following child health interventions: Neglected tropical disease (NTDs). / Polio Eradication Initiative campaign. / Seasonal Malaria Chemoprevention (SMS) [sic, SMC]. / Maternal Newborn and Child health (MNCH).
Leveraging on these platforms will reduce operational costs, maximizes resources, enhance coverage by minimizing missed opportunities and maximizing health benefits to eligible populations.

## p11 (printed 10) AGE ELIGIBILITY FOR AZITHROMYCIN (AZM) ADMINISTRATION DURING CAMPAIGN - no figures
Eligibility age range for Children to receive AZM.
- AZM is given to only healthy children between 1 to 59 months of age.
- Children who are less than one month or older than 59 months are not eligible.
- Children who are 59 months old in round one should receive AZM but will not be eligible in the next round.
- Neonates who are less than one month are not eligible.
WHY ONLY CHILDREN 1 TO 59 MONTHS ARE ELIGIBLE FOR AZM
- The safety protocol for AZM has been established for children 1 months and above.
- Younger children are more susceptible to potential side effects.
- Children between 1 to 59 months are at the highest risk of severe malaria and other childhood infections
- The age group experience the highest mortality rates from childhood infections.
- It is easier to monitor and manager potential side effects in this age group (1-59 months.
What are the ways to determine the age of a child: Ask to see the child's child health card. / Ask the caregiver the age of the child. / Use calendar of events.
1 to 2 months: Cannot hold head and neck steady when held upright.
3 to 4 months: Can hold head and neck steady when held upright.
3 to 11 months: Can hold head and neck steady when held upright. / Can crawl. / Can sit without help.
12 to 59 months: Can stand, walk or run. / Not able to raise the arm and touch the opposite side of the ear. / Not able to stand on one leg.
Older than 59 months: Can raise the arm and touch the opposite side of the ear. / Can stand on one leg for 10 secs. / Can jump on one leg. / They are not eligible for AZM MDA.

## p15 (printed 14) CHILDREN THAT ARE NOT ELIGIBLE FOR AZM DRUG ADMINISTRATION - no figures
Bold: Do not give AZM to children who have taken antibiotics of similar medication in the last 28 days or 4 weeks or one month.
Ask the caregiver: Has the child taken any medicine in the last 28 days or 4 weeks. / If yes, what medicine has the child taken in the past one month. / If possible, ask to see the packet for confirmation.
CHILDREN NOT ELIGIBLE FOR AZM MDA DUE TO MALNUTRITION: How to identify a child with severe acute malnutrition (Look and Feel Approach).
- Visible sever wasting; / The child looks extremely thin. / The ribs, shoulder blades and bones are very prominent. / The arms and thigh look like "skin and bones" with little or no muscle or flesh. / The child's buttock may appear flat or baggy.
Bilateral Pitting Oedema: Press gently with your thumb on the top of both feet for about 3 seconds. / If a pit remains after removing the thumb, oedema is present. / This indicates that the child has Kwashiorkor, a form of SAM.
AZM SHOULD NOT BE GIVEN TO THE FOLLOWING CHILDREN: Children from 60 months and older. / Children that are allergic to AZM or similar antibiotics . / Children who are sick or have fever. / Children who have received AZM or other antibiotics in the last one month. / Children who are currently taking AZM or other antibiotics. / Children who cannot swallow.

## p31 (printed 30) AZITHROMYCIN DRUG ADMINISTRATION PROTOCOL - no figures
EXPLAIN AND SHOW: How to safely administer AZM: The CDD should check the expiry date of drug before reconstitution. / Wash hands with soap and water before administering AZM. / Limit AZM administration to 5 eligible children at a time. / Ask the caregiver to bring drinking water in a small cup.
Steps for administering AZM. First, shake the AZM powder vigorously to loosen it. / Add 15ml of clean water and shake the bottle thoroughly to mix. / For children 1-11 months, determine the dose using the age-based dosing strategy (3-tier dosing schedule) as follows: 1-2 months will receive 2ml, 3-4 months will receive 3ml while 5-11 months will receive 4ml.
For children 12-59 months: The dosing pole will be used to measure their height (Height based dosing strategy). The measurement in cm is on one side of the pole while the corresponding dose to be administered to the child is indicated on the opposite side of the pole. / The DIGIT HCM app will indicate the dose to be administered when the age or height of the child is inputted into the app. / The CDD should measure out the accurate dose of AZM to be administered using the provided dispensing cup. / AZM should be administered to one child per time. / If the child vomits within 5 minutes after receiving AZM, the child should be redosed with the same dose according to their age or height. / Observe the child for 30 minutes after administration for any side effect or Suspected ADR for referral.

## p32 (printed 31) AZITHROMYCIN DRUG ADMINISTRATION PROTOCOL - no figures
How AZM Medicines are issued: OICs in the facilities will issue AZM medicine and other implementation tools to each CDD team at the beginning of each day of the implementation period. / CDD teams must meet the OIC every morning during the implementation period to request for AZM medicine needed for administration to the target population. / Each CDD team is expected to reach a total of 100 children each day during the 4 days distribution period
What need to be returned to the OICs at the end of each day during MDA: All AZM bottles must be accounted for daily during the MDA. / All used and unused bottles of AZM medicines must be returned to the Health facility OIC. / Partially used AZM medicines remain viable for up to 2 to 3 days and must be reused before a new one is opened.
Right col: CDDs must measure the exact dose displayed on the DIGIT app because overdosing or under dosing will result in wastage that will affect drug reconciliation leading to accountability issues, and drug resistance. / CDD must ensure that dosing poles are returned to the health facility daily. / The OICs must check the technology app to know the number of children reached by each CDD team . / The OICs should document the distribution of AZM in the inventory control cards and all the empty bottles should be properly arranged in the cartons.
